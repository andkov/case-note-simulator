# Synthetic Client Archetype Descriptions (Alberta-like)

This set of 10 archetypes spans the required complexity spectrum (Levels 1–4) and aligns with demographic and risk factor prevalence targets. Approximate share % indicates suggested proportional allocation within the 500-profile synthetic cohort (sums ~100%).

---
## Legend
- Complexity Level: 1 = Stable, 2 = Moderate, 3 = High, 4 = Crisis
- Protective Factors: Conditions that moderate risk or support resilience
- Primary Risk Factors draw from: mental health (MH), substance use (SU), housing instability (HI), medical complexity (MC), justice involvement (JI), dependent care (DC), employment barriers (EB)

---
## Archetype A1 – Urban Early Career Stabilizer
- Share: ~8%
- Demographics: Age 25–34, any gender (slight female tilt), single adult, urban (Calgary/Edmonton)
- Primary Risks: Mild employment instability (recent part-time hours), low financial margin
- Protective Factors: Stable housing, no MH/SU issues, digitally engaged, proactive help-seeking
- Complexity Level: 1 (Stable) – Only minor economic vulnerability, few co-occurring risks
- Narrative: Recently transitioned from contract work to part-time employment while seeking full-time placement. Uses supports appropriately, maintains consistent communication, minimal intervention intensity required.

## Archetype A2 – Rural Steady Trades Worker
- Share: ~9%
- Demographics: Age 35–44, male-majority, couple (no dependents), rural small town/remote service area
- Primary Risks: Seasonal employment gaps, transportation barriers for training access
- Protective Factors: Strong partner support, no MH/SU flags, stable owned housing
- Complexity Level: 1 (Stable) – Predictable cyclical barriers but low acute risk profile
- Narrative: Works intermittently in fabrication or resource-adjacent labor; seeks credential upgrade off-season. Requires light coordination for skills development timing.

## Archetype A3 – Older Stable Support-Seeking Couple
- Share: ~8%
- Demographics: Age 55–64, mixed gender couple with adult children living independently, urban mid-size city (Red Deer/Lethbridge)
- Primary Risks: Mild chronic health (hypertension), gradual employability decline
- Protective Factors: Stable rental, strong financial management skills, no acute MH/SU
- Complexity Level: 1 (Stable) – Limited progression of risk factors, low service intensity
- Narrative: Facing late-career transition after light manufacturing displacement. Seeks retraining pathway; remains engaged and organized in documentation.

## Archetype A4 – Single Parent Housing Strain
- Share: ~12%
- Demographics: Age 25–44, female-majority, single parent (2 dependents), urban peripheral neighborhood
- Primary Risks: HI (risk of loss/overcrowding), EB (childcare constraints), financial stress
- Protective Factors: Motivated for training, consistent caseworker engagement
- Complexity Level: 2 (Moderate) – Layered housing + employment barriers without acute MH/SU
- Narrative: Navigates unstable rental with rising costs while pursuing short credential program. Relies on Income Support for stabilization during child care gaps.

## Archetype A5 – Midlife Health & Employment Barriers
- Share: ~11%
- Demographics: Age 45–54, mixed gender distribution, single adult, urban core
- Primary Risks: EB (skills obsolescence), MC (2 chronic conditions), emerging mild MH (anxiety)
- Protective Factors: Treatment adherence, stable subsidized housing
- Complexity Level: 2 (Moderate) – Multiple barriers yet mitigated by stability of housing and engagement
- Narrative: Former clerical worker with degenerative joint issues retraining for remote-compatible roles. Anxiety manageable; benefits from structured planning support.

## Archetype A6 – Urban Transitional Recovery Participant
- Share: ~11%
- Demographics: Age 35–44, any gender (slight male tilt), single adult, urban supportive housing
- Primary Risks: SU (recent treatment), MH (depression history), EB (gap >12 months)
- Protective Factors: Active participation in recovery program, case management adherence, transitional housing
- Complexity Level: 2 (Moderate) – Co-occurring MH/SU but stabilized through structured supports
- Narrative: Six months post-intensive outpatient program; pursuing incremental re-entry through volunteer placement and skills workshops.

## Archetype A7 – Rural Multi-Role Caregiver
- Share: ~11%
- Demographics: Age 35–54, female-majority, multi-generational household (elder + 1 child), rural
- Primary Risks: EB (caregiving load), DC, HI (crowding risk), limited service access
- Protective Factors: Strong informal family network, no SU, mild MH only (stress)
- Complexity Level: 2 (Moderate) – Multiple social determinants elevate coordination need without acute crisis markers
- Narrative: Balances elder support and child developmental needs while exploring remote learning options. Requires navigation assistance for transport and respite.

## Archetype A8 – Multi-Factor Urban Instability Case
- Share: ~13%
- Demographics: Age 25–44, any gender, single adult, urban precarious housing (rooming/couch surfing)
- Primary Risks: HI (precarious), MH (anxiety + intermittent depressive episodes), EB (long-term unemployment), occasional low-level JI (past probation)
- Protective Factors: Intermittent peer support, motivated for stabilization
- Complexity Level: 3 (High) – Dense clustering of instability factors with partial engagement
- Narrative: Cycles between short-term placements and informal living arrangements; struggles with sustained program attendance. Needs coordinated multi-service plan.

## Archetype A9 – Chronic Medical & Mental Health Complexity
- Share: ~12%
- Demographics: Age 55–64, any gender, single or separated, urban mid-size city
- Primary Risks: MC (2–3 chronic conditions + recent hospital utilization), MH (persistent depression), EB (functional limitations)
- Protective Factors: Regular primary care linkage, medication adherence when supported
- Complexity Level: 3 (High) – High medical + MH burden driving sustained service intensity
- Narrative: Frequent care navigation challenges and episodic functional decline undermine employment re-engagement. Requires integrated health-social coordination.

## Archetype A10 – Acute Housing & Co-Occurring Crisis
- Share: ~5%
- Demographics: Age 25–34 or 35–44, any gender, single adult, urban core shelter / street context
- Primary Risks: HI (homeless), SU (active), MH (deterioration pattern), recent JI (within 12–18 months), EB severe
- Protective Factors: Early engagement with outreach team, occasional motivation spikes
- Complexity Level: 4 (Crisis) – Multiple acute, interacting destabilizers requiring immediate, multi-agency intervention
- Narrative: Recently disengaged from transitional program; presents with escalating symptoms and high volatility in service adherence. Priority triage for stabilization and risk containment.

## Complexity Distribution Alignment
- Level 1 (Stable): A1–A3 → ~25%
- Level 2 (Moderate): A4–A7 → ~45%
- Level 3 (High): A8–A9 → ~25%
- Level 4 (Crisis): A10 → ~5%

Distribution matches project calibration targets and supports scenario-specific validation (housing crisis, MH deterioration, service coordination outcomes).

---
## Usage Guidance
- Assign profiles proportionally; then probabilistically layer exact risk factor flags using prevalence & co-occurrence tables.
- Persona narratives inform writing style modulation (e.g., concise for stable, fragmented for crisis).
- Protective factors can drive generation of positive service connection outcomes (target 12%).
