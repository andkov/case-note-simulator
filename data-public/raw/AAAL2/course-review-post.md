During Oct 14-24, it was my pleasure to take part in #AAAL2, the second in the three-part course series developed by the #AlbertaAIAcademy of the #Ablerta Technology and Innovation. 

Building on #AAAL1, which mostly dealth with prompting techniques and AI literacy, the second course dived into agentic space, orchestration, and automation, showcasing 5 (!!!) systems:
- Agent Builder Console (ABC, developed in-house by TI)
- n8n 
- Amazon AWS Bedrock
- Google Gemini Enterprise
- Microsoft Copilot Studio. 

Overview of various vendors was immensely helpful for getting a lay of the land of the rapidly changing landscape of generative AI. That alone was worth way more than the price of admission = two week of protected time (plus forelog and backlog, ugh). 

But what had a beyond-measurable value was the reality check against ~120+ folks from GoA and other public service, meeting chat banter, working lunches over coffee and pizza (thanks Michelle Diaz!), and the gradually receding feeling of NOT having to eat the elephant of the AI adoption alone.

And now to the reason why you are still reading this: which system was better? 

I'm relatively new to AI agents, so in my books "better" means "helping to learn system thinking for design of agents, orchestration of their cooperation, and automation of workflows". What helped me LEARN the most was, hands down, ABC, with n8n a close second. 

While the three giants (Amazon, Google, Microsoft) presented a much more encompassing, more powerful solutions, it seemed that they were burdened by their sprawling stacks and expectations of full enterprise integration, and that slowed them down when it got to getting people aboard, overcoming the initial learning curve. In contrast, ABC kept things simple, prioritized human learning, and provided amazing support network. True, n8n was a better, more nimble, prettier tool for more general applications, and if I had to chose a single tool between these two, I'd have to pick n8n for its versatility.
